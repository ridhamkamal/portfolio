Simplified: a 120-word known-vocabulary list rather than the child’s actual learned-word set, and a Flesch-Kincaid estimate rather than the full library. The thresholds and the pass/fail behaviour are the production ones.

Formulas, assumptions and exclusions

Monthly revenue is users × DIY rate × $9.99, plus users × Family rate × $17.99. Gross margin is revenue less paying users × cost to serve, over revenue.

Monthly billing only: the annual plan and its 17% discount are excluded, so real blended margin would differ. Cost to serve is one blended figure covering inference, speech compute, hosting and storage. It excludes support, payment processing and acquisition. Pricing and the 47–61% margin band come from the August 2026 pricing strategy. These are modeled projections, not booked revenue.

About

The same job under different constraints

01

The buyer is not always the user

At Juniper, engineers use it and leadership buys it. In WonderWord, the child uses it and the parent pays. Both demand a product that proves its value to someone who never touches it.

02

Constraints inform the spec

Enterprise compliance and COPPA are the same discipline at different stakes. Treating the regulatory boundary as the architecture, rather than a policy page bolted on afterward, is the habit that transfers.

03

Trust before capability

A model that is right 90% of the time and honest about the rest beats one that is right 95% and silent. That held for enterprise recommendations and it holds harder for a six-year-old.

AI Product Manager with eight years delivering AI-enabled, data-driven and enterprise software products. I own the full lifecycle for generative AI, LLM and RAG, analytics and intelligent automation: discovery, vision, roadmaps, PRDs, user stories, success metrics, evaluation and launch.

What connects the enterprise work to a children’s reading app is the constraint. At Juniper the boundary is compliance and interoperability; in WonderWord it is COPPA and a six-year-old’s confidence. In both, the regulatory line is the specification, not an afterthought, and the product only works if the person in the workflow trusts what the model says.
