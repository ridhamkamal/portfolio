01

Context and target user

An AI retrieval layer for network-operations teams triaging alerts. It surfaces how similar past incidents were handled so operators are not re-solving problems someone already solved.

Data source

Built and evaluated on public system-log data. No employer data anywhere in it.

02

Problem and evidence

Ops teams drown in alert volume and re-solve incidents someone already solved last quarter. The scarce resource is attention, not data.

Not documented in the source portfolio. Quantitative evidence on alert volume or time spent; the prototype uses public logs.

03

My role and team

Not documented in the source portfolio. The specific role and team for this concept. Artifacts produced: a product brief, a phase-one PRD with acceptance criteria and a working prototype.

04

Constraints

  • Asymmetric cost of error. Missing a critical alert costs far more than a false positive, so automation must earn trust step by step.
  • Public data only. No employer data.
05

Product hypothesis

Retrieving remediations from past incidents can cut the attention operators spend on alerts that have been solved before — provided automation is introduced gradually and can be withdrawn.

Framed from the documented problem statement.

06

Alternatives and tradeoffs

The retrieval layer scored well on the first evaluation. How do you measure whether it actually works?

  • Cumulative accuracy across the full dataset — the original method
  • Human spot-check of a sample
  • A chronological split, training on earlier incidents and testing on later ones — chosen

Tradeoff. The corrected number was worse, and it changed the product’s phasing and requirements. I had a good result and chose to invalidate it.

07

Key decisions

  • Automation ladder with promotion and demotion thresholds committed in advance.
  • Metrics tree carrying a counter-metric and a hard-stop guardrail, because the cost of a missed critical alert is asymmetric.
  • Re-ran retrieval evaluation as a chronological split after the cumulative measure proved misleading.

09

Evaluation and success metrics

The cumulative measure let the system retrieve remediations that, in production, would not have existed yet. It was scoring well by looking at the future. The chronological split fixes that leak.

The metrics tree pairs the primary measure with a counter-metric and a hard-stop guardrail; the automation ladder promotes or demotes automation against thresholds set before results were seen.

Not documented in the source portfolio. The before-and-after evaluation numbers and the threshold values.

10

Validation status

Concept · Prototyped

The corrected evaluation changed the product’s phasing and requirements. Not deployed.

11

Lessons and next steps

  • If an offline metric looks strong, check whether the split leaks time. This is the correct method for anything retrieving from a growing incident history.
  • Commit automation thresholds in advance, so promotion and demotion are not argued after the fact.
