01

Context and target user

An AI recruiting product for the people who run interview loops: recruiters, interviewers and hiring managers. It connects interview preparation, panel alignment and feedback into one workflow.

02

Problem and evidence

Discovery across recruiter, interviewer and hiring-manager personas found the real gap was coordination, not judgment. No system connected preparation, panel alignment and feedback, so each happened in isolation and the loop never closed.

Not documented in the source portfolio. Interview counts, quotes or quantitative findings from discovery. See the Miro board for the underlying research.

03

My role and team

Not documented in the source portfolio. The specific role and team composition for this concept. Artifacts produced: discovery, a 22-section PRD, a Miro board and a working prototype.

04

Constraints

  • Legal exposure and fairness risk. Any AI that evaluates candidates creates decisions the product cannot defend.
  • Compliance review. The product had to survive one — which shaped the AI boundary below.

Responsible AI boundary

AI summarizes approved information and suggests job-related questions. It does not score candidates, recommend hires, or write interviewer feedback.

05

Product hypothesis

Because the gap is coordination, connecting the three stages — Prepare → Align → Close the loop — should get interview loops to complete, evidence-backed feedback faster, without the AI making judgments about candidates.

Framed from the documented problem statement and north-star metric.

06

Alternatives and tradeoffs

How far should the AI go with access to candidate data and interview feedback?

  • Score and rank candidates
  • Draft feedback for interviewers
  • Recommend hire or no-hire with a confidence level
  • Summarize approved data and suggest job-related questions only — chosen

Tradeoff. Scoring is the feature buyers ask for in demos and the one that creates legal exposure, fairness risk and a product that makes decisions it cannot defend. I gave up obvious demo appeal for a product that survives a compliance review. The scoring capability solved a problem nobody named.

07

Key decisions

  • Prepare → Align → Close the loop as the core workflow.
  • Panel coverage-gap detection during alignment.
  • Evidence-required feedback when closing the loop.
  • Role-based views for each persona.
  • A 22-section PRD with MoSCoW scope, user stories, acceptance criteria and a V1 to V3 rollout.

Evaluation and success metrics

  • North star: 70% of interview loops with complete feedback inside 24 hours. Target
  • Counter-metrics: time-to-hire and fairness.
10

Validation status

Concept · Prototyped

Not documented in the source portfolio. User testing of the prototype or measured results against the north star.

11

Lessons and next steps

  • Next: the PRD defines a V1 to V3 rollout.
  • What would change my mind on scoring: nothing in the current regulatory environment. If an auditable, jurisdiction-cleared fairness standard existed and a customer required scoring, I would revisit it as an explicitly human-reviewed suggestion, never an automated decision.

Not documented in the source portfolio. A written retrospective of lessons learned.
