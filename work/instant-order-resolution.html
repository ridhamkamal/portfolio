01

Context and target user

A delivery-marketplace product concept for the moment an order goes wrong. The analysis positions Uber Eats as the challenger to DoorDash and asks where a challenger can build a defensible advantage. The target user is the customer whose order has failed.

02

Problem and evidence

When a delivery order goes wrong, resolution is slow and the customer has decided how they feel long before support replies.

Dated research · from the case study

Roughly 24% US share

Uber Eats as the number-two player at the time of the analysis.

Dated research · from the case study

88% vs 98% order accuracy

Uber Eats trails DoorDash on order accuracy. The failure moment happens more often — and is worth more to fix.

Market figures reflect the case study’s research at the time it was written and are not presented as current facts.

03

My role and team

Not documented in the source portfolio. The specific role and team for this concept. Artifacts produced: a competitive analysis, a full PRD and an interactive prototype.

04

Constraints

  • Challenger economics. Selection and speed are won with capital, price is won with margin — none available to the challenger.
  • Abuse. Fast resolution invites refund abuse, so guardrails are part of the core mechanic.
  • Cost attribution. Who pays for a failed order has to be explicit.
05

Product hypothesis

Reframing the starting hypothesis. The analysis overturned its own premise: the widely repeated claim that DoorDash is losing share is inverted. Reframing around Uber Eats as challenger changed which strategies were even available.

The resulting hypothesis: post-failure order resolution is the defensible wedge, supported by a cross-subsidy advantage from rides that DoorDash cannot replicate.

06

Alternatives and tradeoffs

  • Match DoorDash on restaurant selection
  • Compete on delivery speed
  • Undercut on pricing and fees
  • Own post-failure order resolution — chosen

Tradeoff. The three obvious options all require outspending a larger incumbent on its own terms.

07

Key decisions

  • Pre-authorization mechanics to resolve failed orders instantly.
  • Abuse guardrails built into the resolution flow.
  • Cost attribution for each failure.
  • Success gated on operational accuracy rather than CSAT.

Evaluation and success metrics

Success criteria are tied to operational accuracy rather than customer-satisfaction scores, so the feature is judged on whether failed orders are actually resolved correctly.

Not documented in the source portfolio. The specific metric definitions and thresholds. These are in the linked PRD.

Validation status

11

Lessons and next steps

  • Check the premise first. The analysis overturned its starting claim, and the reframing changed the strategy set.
  • What would change my mind: if Uber Eats closed the accuracy gap the wedge would shrink, because the feature’s value scales with how often orders fail. I would also abandon it if the cross-subsidy were regulated away.
